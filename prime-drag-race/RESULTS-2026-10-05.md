## What this does and does not show

- It shows a correct, reproducible measurement of one small workload on a Linux-hosted GB10, with receipts anyone can re-hash.
- A prime sieve result is not overall AIEN performance. It says nothing about AIENOS, models, or other workloads.
- On this workload the native GB10 path is about 20x slower than CUDA running the same algorithm (median ratio 20.2x), and slower than the single-thread CPU baseline (median ratio 2.13x).
- Run-to-run spread is small (CV under 1% for all three in the final run: see table), so the gaps are not noise.

DIAGNOSTIC, not a benchmark result. Source: omega `bench/prime_race/tests/gb10_launch_cost.c` (commit dc7a3d7). Logs: `~/workspace/investigations/2026-10-05-prime-sieve-chip/runs/e4.log` and `e5.log` (local investigation folder, not in this repo). 200 launches each, time from submit to marker2:

| sieve limit 1000 | 103.8 us |

CUDA kernel-only time for comparison: about 53 us (cudaEvent, diagnostic).

- OBSERVED: kernel time minus the ~104 us launch floor is 3356, 1729 and 924 us at 16, 32 and 64 CTAs. It about halves each time the CTA count doubles, so the kernel is latency-bound.
- INFERRED, improvement 1: occupancy. The 64-CTA launch cap (omega open investigation I42) and only 4 warps per CTA limit how much latency can be hidden.
- INFERRED, improvement 2: the emitter fully serializes instructions and waits on every uncached global load (167 table loads per word). Caching or staging the table and overlapping loads is general code generation work.
- INFERRED, improvement 3: a fixed cost of about 104 us per launch (shader-cache invalidate, L2 flush, two markers; the marker wait polls with usleep(50)). That is about 10% of one pass.
